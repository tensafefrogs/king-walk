Privacy Policy

Last updated November 21, 2026. This covers King Walk on the web, the King Walk app for iPhone and iPad, and the King Walk app for Android.

King Walk has no accounts, ads or in-app purchases, and collects nothing that identifies you.

Your progress

Your puzzle rating, history and settings are saved on your device, including how you do at each kind of puzzle (forks, pins and so on), worked out on the device. In the iOS app, a copy is also kept in your own iCloud account (Apple's iCloud key-value storage), so your progress follows you to your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account. The developer can't see this copy; Apple stores it under its own privacy policy.

In the Android app, your progress is included in your phone's own backup, if backup is turned on in the phone's settings, so a new phone, or the app installed again, starts where you left off. Google keeps the backup in your Google Account under its own privacy policy; the developer can't see it.

The widgets read the same progress from the app on your device. Nothing leaves your device for it.

If you turn on the daily reminder, the app schedules it on your device. The reminder doesn't go through any server, and you can turn it off in the game's settings or in your phone's settings.

To delete your progress: Menu → Start over erases it on this device, and in the iOS app also its iCloud copy. Deleting the app, or clearing this site's data in your browser, removes it from that device.

Game Center and Google Play Games

In the iOS app, if you are signed in to Game Center, the app sends your scores (puzzle rating, streaks, daily streak and hardest puzzle solved) to the King Walk leaderboards, and unlocks achievements as you play. Game Center is run by Apple: what other players can see depends on your Game Center settings, and Apple handles it under its own privacy policy. You can sign out of Game Center in the Settings app, and the game works the same without it.

In the Android app, the same scores and achievements go to Google Play Games, if you use it. Google runs Play Games under its own privacy policy, and your Play Games settings decide what others see. The game works the same without it. Google Play also handles the app's updates and the rating prompt.

Anonymous play counts

The game counts anonymous events, for example how many people start, how many reach 10 or 100 puzzles, which rank they reach, which kind of device they play on (such as iPhone, iPad, Android or a computer), which language the game is shown in (and the device's language, when the game doesn't have it), how often hints are used, and which kinds of puzzle are practised and how those practice sets go (not your results by kind, which stay on your device). It uses GoatCounter, which doesn't use cookies, doesn't store IP addresses (it uses them only to estimate the country) and doesn't track you across apps or websites.

The counts are totals, not records about a person, so there is nothing that could be linked to you, looked up or deleted for you. They aren't shared or sold. If you'd rather not send them, play offline: nothing is sent then, and content blockers stop them in a browser.

Links to other sites

Each puzzle links to its page on Lichess, where the puzzle comes from. Links open in your browser, and those sites have their own privacy policies.

Children

King Walk is suitable for all ages and collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes and questions

If this policy changes, this page will say so and show a new date. Questions: @tensafefrogs on X.
