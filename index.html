How it works
  • Each puzzle starts with the other side's move. Then it is your turn.
  • Tap a piece, then tap where it goes. You can also drag it.
  • Solve a puzzle with no mistakes and no hints and your puzzle rating goes up. The next puzzles get harder.
  • Miss one and your rating goes down a little. The next puzzles get easier.
  • The first hint circles the piece to move. The second circles where it goes. Solving with a hint counts as half.
  • A missed puzzle comes back once, about 30 puzzles later, for a second try. Second tries do not change your rating.
  • Progress is saved in this browser only. There are no accounts.
  • The name: a king walk is when a king marches up the board to help win the game. You start at Pawn rank and work your way up to King.

Progress

Ranks

You start as a Pawn. As your puzzle rating goes up you earn the next rank: Knight at 700, Bishop at 1000, Rook at 1300, Queen at 1600 and King at 1900.

Your puzzle rating

Every puzzle has a number that says how hard it is. You have a number too. Solve a puzzle and you win some points. Miss one and you give some back.

Hard puzzles are worth more

Beating a puzzle rated above you earns a lot of points. Beating an easy one earns only a few. Missing an easy one costs more than missing a hard one.

It finds your level

The game keeps giving you puzzles close to your number. When you are solving a little more than half of them, your number settles. That is your level right now. Get better and it climbs again.

Where this comes from

This way of rating is called Elo, after Arpad Elo, who invented it to rank chess players. Yours is a puzzle rating on the Lichess puzzle scale, so it usually reads higher than a rating earned by playing full games.
