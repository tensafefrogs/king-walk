King Walk

Chess puzzles that fight back.

King Walk throws real chess puzzles at you, 53,000 of them, pulled from actual games. Solve one and the next gets tougher. Miss one and it backs off. A few dozen puzzles in, it knows exactly how good you are. Then it starts pushing.

Players start as a Pawn and fight their way up through Knight, Bishop, Rook and Queen to King. Rank streaks raise the stakes: 15 puzzles, no hints, one miss and you're out. Friends can challenge each other through Game Center. The name comes from chess: a king walk is when a king marches up the board to win the game, as in Short vs. Timman, Tilburg 1991.

Features

  • 53,000 puzzles from real games: forks, pins, skewers, back rank mates, mates in one, two and three, endgames and more
  • A puzzle rating that follows the player's level (Elo), with a chart of their progress
  • Two-step hints, and missed puzzles that come back later for a second try
  • A daily puzzle, the same for everyone, with a daily streak and a spoiler-free emoji result to share
  • Rank streaks: 15 puzzles that climb through a rank with no hints, and Game Center challenges to race friends through them
  • Easier, Just right or Harder, to set the pace
  • iPhone and iPad: Game Center leaderboards and 22 achievements, Home Screen, Lock Screen and StandBy widgets, a Control Center control, Siri and Shortcuts, an optional daily reminder, iCloud sync, haptics
  • Share your progress as a few emoji: rank, rating and the last ten puzzles
  • On iPhone Duo, prop it up like a laptop or hold it like a book, with the board on whichever side suits your hand
  • In 11 languages; light and dark mode; plays offline
  • No account, no ads, no in-app purchases

About the puzzles

The puzzles are a sample of the Lichess puzzle database, which Lichess released into the public domain: 3,000 per 100-point rating band from 400 to 1999 and 1,000 per band from 2000 to 2499, limited to well-tested, well-liked puzzles. Their ratings come from how real players did on them. King Walk is not made or endorsed by Lichess, a free, donation-funded chess site. Chess rules come from chess.js (BSD license).
